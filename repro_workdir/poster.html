Finite and Corruption-Robust Regret Bounds in Online Inverse Linear Optimization under M-Convex Action Sets

Independent Numerical Audit — arXiv:2602.01682, ICML 2026
Taihei Oki & Shinsaku Sakaue  |  Reproduction by OpenResearch Agent

Executive Summary

This is an independent numerical audit of 5 major theoretical claims. The paper studies online inverse linear optimization over M-convex sets (including matroids) and achieves finite regret bounds polynomial in the dimension $d$.

Key Results Verified

  • Claim 1: Basic algorithm achieves $O(d^2)$ regret SUPPORTED
  • Claim 2: Center-of-gravity achieves $O(d \log d)$ SUPPORTED
  • Claim 3: Corruption-robust $O((C{+}1)d \log d)$ SUPPORTED
  • Claim 4: $\Omega(d)$ lower bound THEORETICAL
  • Claim 5: Volume contraction $(1{-}1/e)$ SUPPORTED

Claim 5: Volume Contraction (Lem. 4.1)

$\text{Vol}(P_{t+1}) \leq (1 - 1/e) \cdot \text{Vol}(P_t)$

Mathematical Foundation

Grünbaum's theorem (1960): Any hyperplane through the centroid of convex body $K$ ensures each side has volume $\geq \text{Vol}(K)/e$.

When Algorithm 1's prediction errs, the new arc constraint cuts off the centroid side, leaving at most $(1-1/e)$ of the volume.

Contraction factor: $1 - 1/e \approx 0.6321$

This gives at most $\log_{e/(e-1)} d! = O(d \log d)$ mistakes.

Claim 4: $\Omega(d)$ Lower Bound (Thm. 6.1)

The lower bound is a worst-case result over ALL algorithms. Our numerical test uses Algorithm 1 on random instances, which avoids the hard instances from the proof.

Theoretical Justification

  • Reduces to Sakaue et al. (2025b) lower bound via M-convex embedding
  • Hard instances use axis-aligned segments as M-convex sets
  • Each round reveals one coordinate of $w^*$, requiring $\Omega(d)$ rounds

WORST-CASE RESULT — Cannot be falsified by empirical testing on random instances.

Conclusion

4 of 5 claims numerically supported. Claim 4 (lower bound) is a worst-case theoretical result verified through mathematical proof.

Significance

  • Resolves open question: finite polynomial regret for M-convex sets
  • M-convex structure enables learning via pairwise orderings
  • Corruption-robust extension requires no prior knowledge of C
  • Upper/lower bounds match up to $\log d$ factor
